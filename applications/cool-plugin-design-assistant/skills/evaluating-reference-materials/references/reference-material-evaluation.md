# Reference Material evaluation contract

Evaluate only the material and metadata actually supplied or inspected. For
each candidate, record:

- stable material identifier and source label;
- evidence basis: `inspected content`, `supplied metadata`, or `unverified`;
- knowledge topics and relevance to the approved mission and behaviors;
- structure, format, and suitability for normalization;
- stated authority, author, publisher, version/date, and their verification
  status;
- known limitations, ambiguity, bias, staleness, and scope boundaries;
- reusability and expected behavioral impact;
- provenance chain and attribution requirements;
- rights status separately for inspection, internal transformation,
  redistribution, exact quotation, and generated derivatives;
- conflicts and duplication with other candidates;
- missing coverage and private, personal, secret, or regulated content; and
- whether the intended use requires exact quotation, exact structured
  retrieval, or advisory consultation.

Use `confirmed`, `unresolved`, or `prohibited` for each rights decision. Never
infer permission from possession, public accessibility, unknown authorship, or
the caller's request. If content is not available, evaluate only the supplied
description and label all content-level conclusions provisional.

For overlaps, identify the common knowledge, material differences, authority
differences, and whether one source supersedes another. Do not merge conflicting
claims silently. For gaps, name the unsupported workflow, module, decision, or
output and the kind of evidence needed; do not invent replacement knowledge.

Exact quotation requires inspected wording plus confirmed permission or another
documented legal basis. Advisory status permits the application to consult and
paraphrase approved knowledge within stated limits; it does not authorize
packaging or redistribution.
